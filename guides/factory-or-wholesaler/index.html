Buying Christmas decorations from a factory or a wholesaler: what changes?

The Teck Tai team · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 24 November 2026

Short answer

A wholesaler sells from stock in your country: smaller, mixed orders, delivered in season, with the import handled for you. A factory makes your order for the season ahead: you plan earlier and order more of each design, and in return you can have your own designs, colours and packaging, and you work with the people who make the product.

The difference at a glance

WholesalerFactory
Order sizeSmall, mixed orders from stockLarger quantities per design, colour and packaging
When you orderIn season, from what is in stockMonths ahead of the season
RangeWhat the wholesaler has chosen to stockThe factory’s collection, or designs developed to your brief
Your own designs and packagingRarelyYes: colours, finishes and retail packaging under your brand
ImportDone for youYour responsibility, or your forwarder’s
Who you talk toA team selling many brandsThe people who develop and make the product

When a wholesaler is the better choice

If you need small quantities of many different items, want to reorder during the season, or don’t want to handle freight and customs, a wholesaler in your own country is usually the simpler route. You sell the same products as other customers of the same wholesaler.

When buying direct makes sense

Buying from the factory makes sense when you plan your Christmas range ahead, order enough of each design, and want something your competitors don’t have: your own colours, a coordinated collection, or packaging with your brand on it. Importers, wholesale distributors and retail buyers who build their own ranges usually buy this way.

What changes when you buy direct

  • Planning: you choose the range and confirm the order months before the season. See when to order Christmas decorations for 2027.
  • Samples: you approve samples before production starts.
  • Packaging: you approve the packaging artwork and format, from clear boxes to counter displays.
  • Compliance: you check the factory’s social audit and ask for the documents your market needs. See what an amfori BSCI audit covers.
  • Shipping: you agree the shipping terms with the factory and book freight, usually through a forwarder.

Questions to ask a factory before the first order

  1. Do you make the products in your own factory, or do you source them from others?
  2. Which social audit do you hold, and is it current?
  3. Can you develop colours, finishes and packaging to our brief?
  4. Who will be our contact from samples to shipment?
  5. By when do we need to confirm the order for delivery in time for our season?
